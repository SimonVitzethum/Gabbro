# Audit of the whole tree, 2026-09-01

**What this is:** one pass over everything, looking for defects, for ways the language could be
better, and ending in a judgement of the language itself. Every number below names its
denominator. Where I measured wrongly, that stands here too — three times, and the third one
changed a conclusion.

Measured at `23837ca`. All runs local, `free -g`: 31 GB total, 13 GB available, 20 cores.

## 2 What holds, and it is more than I expected

### 2.1 Every poison probe fires the code it claims — 370 of 370

Each file under `beispiele/gift/` names an expected code in its first line. Run each through the
tool that owns that code, and **370 of 370 fire it**. Three files carry no expectation.

*My first run of this said `19 red`, and that was my own error* — I measured `C001` with `gabbro
pruefe`, and `C001` is the **emitter's** refusal. `W16`, in the auditor's hand.

### 2.2 The emitted C is tighter than the house gate demands

The house compiles with `-Wall -Wextra -Werror`. Over all 63 emitted units:

| tool | findings |
|---|---:|
| `-Wconversion -Wsign-conversion -Wshadow -Wcast-qual -Wstrict-overflow=5` | **0** |
| GCC `-fanalyzer` | **0** |
| `clang -Weverything` (minus three pure style flags) | 205 |

`-Wconversion` is the C analogue of `M104` — an implicit narrowing — and there is **not one**.
Of the 205 clang findings, 74 are unused macros, 36 are unreachable code after a diverging call,
12 are `switch` without `default:` (**which is Gabbro's exhaustive `match` lowered faithfully**),
and one is a `goto` that C++ would dislike and C does not — verified by hand at
`39-auftragsdienst.c:147`, the jumped-over variable is dead at the label.

**The remaining 54 are `-Wcast-align`, and they are §3.1.**

### 2.3 The proof layer has no holes *in itself*

101 theorems over 15 theories. **Zero `sorry`, zero `oops`, zero `axiomatization`.**
Whether their premises are established by a pass is a different question, and the repo already
measures it: 87 templates claim 215 of 263 identifiers, 48 stand without one.

### 2.4 Depth is refused, not survived

Nested parentheses at 100 / 1 000 / 5 000 / 20 000 all end in `P038`, *"nesting deeper than …"*
— a named refusal, at every depth, with no stack overflow. A recursive-descent parser that
answers 20 000 levels with a sentence is doing the right thing.

### 2.5 The lowering is close to 1:1

3 093 non-comment Gabbro lines emit 3 334 non-comment C lines: **1.08**. For a language whose
trust base is its emitter this is the load-bearing number — *the reviewer of the emitted C reads
about as much as the author wrote.*

Throughput, unoptimised debug binary: 63 files, 6 206 lines, **325 ms** — 5.2 ms per file.

## 3 What was found — three refusals that were missing

All three are in the MMIO layout, all three are the same shape, and **all three came out of the
emitted C rather than out of a reading.**

### 3.1 `N047` — a register offset need not be aligned to its own width

`clang -Wcast-align` reports 54 casts from `volatile uint8_t *` to a wider type. Every one is an
MMIO access; on the corpus every one is in fact aligned — **and nothing held it there:**

```
reg KRUMM : u64 @0x04 class rw          ->  0 errors, 0 hints
return (*(volatile uint64_t *)(d->basis + 4));
```

Undefined behaviour under **C11 6.3.2.3p7** the moment `basis` is 8-aligned — and it must be, or
the other registers are wrong. It **faults** on aarch64 and riscv. On x86 it splits into two bus
transactions, *which is not one register read.*

> A language whose reason for existing is that MMIO addresses are provably right accepted an
> address that is provably wrong.

### 3.2 `N048` — a bank register need not fit its own cell

`N009` refuses two registers that overlap. `N010` refuses `stride 0`. Between them:

```
bank F at 0x100 stride 4 count 8 { reg X : u64 @0x0 class rw }    ->  0 errors
return *(volatile uint64_t *)(d->basis + (256) + i * 4u + 0u);
```

`F[0].X` reads eight bytes at `basis+0x100`, `F[1].X` eight at `basis+0x104`. **They overlap by
four.** `N009` compares registers against *each other* and never against the stride, so a bank
aliased itself in silence.

### 3.3 `N049` — a bank may cover bytes something else already names

`N009` writes down why it stays out of this: *"a bank sits at a COMPUTED base; holding it against
the main level would mean guessing the base."* **A good reason — and it stops applying the moment
the base is a literal.** Then `d.C` and `d.F[0].X` are both `basis + 256`, with 0 errors, while
`class`, `mirrors`, `transition` and every effect list above them reason about two places.

**On its first run this rule found `dokumente/FRAGMENTE.md:817` — the virtio queue.** The block
carried `count 256` three times while its offsets are those of a queue of size **16**: at 256,
`DESC` alone covers `0x000..0x1000`, so every other register and bank in that device lies inside
it. At 16 the arithmetic closes exactly, and it is the classic split virtqueue. *The offsets were
right; the count was not.*

### 3.4 What was measured and is NOT a defect

- **`count 0`** — a bank with no cells. Every access falls at `M103` (*"the index has `u8 in 0 ..
  0`, the array has 0 elements"*). A declaration nobody can use, refused at every use. By Regel A
  it gets no construct: **no refusal without a measured defect.**
- **an offset beyond 4 GiB**, **`u8` at an odd offset** — correct as accepted.
- The 54 `-Wcast-align` sites **stay**. They are now provably aligned, but the emitter does not
  tell the C compiler so. Saying it — an access through an aligned pointer type instead of
  `uint8_t *` — is emitter work and not a checker finding.

## 4 The structural weakness: the checker's own catch-all

Gabbro forbids its users a catch-all branch. `parse.rs` answers the word `switch` with *"`match`
is exhaustive and has no catch-all branch"*, and `D005` refuses a non-exhaustive `match` over a
`tagged type`. **Its own checker holds 359 `_ =>` arms**, 292 of them over the five AST enums.

The measurement that matters is not the count of arms but **how much of the checker a new
language form forces the compiler to name.** Insert a probe variant, build, count `error[E0004]`:

| enum | variants | forced sites | files named |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `ExprArt` | 14 | 14 | emit 7, refinement 2, lib 2, m1, lean, kosten |
| `StmtArt` | 17 | 15 | blindstellen, emit, kosten, lean, lib, m1, pflichten, zeugnis |
| `ItemArt` | 23 | 9 | emit 5, bindung 2, zeugnis, lib |
| `TypExpr` | 11 | 6 | emit 2, umgebung, namen, lib, alias |
| `WirkungArt` | 10 | 4 | geteilt, emit, bindung, alias |

The repo has an answer to this and it is a good one: **the exhaustive walkers in `lib.rs`** are
the one choke point, and a pass's `_ =>` means *"the walker handed me this node and I decline"*,
not *"I never saw it"*. `zeugnis.rs` names **all 23** `ItemArt` variants — the certificate is the
place that must be complete, and it is.

**What does not exist is anything that measures whether that answer still holds.** The class is
not hypothetical; it has fired three times: *"78 holes of one build, each pass forgetting a
different arm"* (`lib.rs:457`), `LeanReason::ALL` as a hand-written `[LeanReason; 32]` beside 33
variants, and a `_ => "traverse"` in `zeugnis.rs` that pulled five of nine domains onto the wrong
answer (removed 2026-08-31). *A separate lane is checking these numbers and judging them per
enum; the table above is mine and unverified.*

## 5 Where I measured wrongly, three times

Because the house rule is that the instrument gets checked before the object.

1. **`19 of 370 poison probes red.**" I ran `gabbro pruefe` on files whose expected code is
   `C001`, the **emitter's** refusal. Correct figure: 370 of 370. *`W16`.*
2. **`ItemArt` forces 2 sites, `TypExpr` 1, `WirkungArt` 2.** The probe variant broke
   `gabbro-syntax`, so **`gabbro-check` was never compiled** — I measured `ast.rs` and called it
   the checker. Correct: 9, 6, 4. *`CLAUDE.md`: a measurement that stops at the first hit
   measures the wrong question.*
3. **The automated retry for (2) inserted `ZzProbeVariante => …` without its `EnumName::`
   prefix** — a resolution error, not an exhaustiveness error, which silently ended the
   measurement and reported zeros. *Same class, one layer down.*

And one process error: I gave two lanes the **main working tree** as their path instead of their
own directory. Both wrote into it while I did, which is the collision `CLAUDE.md` names by name.
Nothing was lost — each of us staged by explicit path — but one lane's `TODO.md` correction
landed in a commit of mine, under a message about something else.

## 6 The judgement

### What Gabbro is

A systems language that emits C, in which **every property it promises is either checked by a
pass or named as not checked.** The second half is the unusual one. `gabbro pruefe` prints, for
every run, which of its passes did *not* look and what remains outside them — nine "CARRIED"
paragraphs, each naming its own residue. Most languages tell you what they checked. This one
tells you what it did not.

### What it does well, ranked by how hard it is to get elsewhere

1. **The refusals are readable.** 260 codes, each with a note that names the *form* to write
   instead. Measured by another lane this week: a fresh reader wrote a correct two-argument
   function from the tutorial in **1 attempt**, with `gabbro costs` run on a draft first.
2. **The trust base is small and dependency-free.** 60 k lines, no crates, and a lowering of
   1.08 C lines per Gabbro line. One can *read* what one is trusting.
3. **The instruments outnumber the excuses.** 29 k lines of guards, several of which measure the
   checker against its own claims rather than against a wish list. Two of them found real defects
   this week — one caught a subcommand that no single lane could have seen was unregistered.
4. **It refuses to guess.** `C001` is the emitter saying *"no lowering for this"* rather than
   emitting something plausible. That is the single decision most responsible for §2.2.

### What is genuinely weak

1. **The hardware layer was under-checked in exactly the place it advertises.** Three missing
   refusals, all in MMIO layout, all found in one afternoon by running the emitted C through
   tools the house does not run. That is not a bad ratio for an audit — it is a bad sign for the
   claim that this area was finished.
2. **Growth of the language is not held by the compiler** for three of five AST enums (§4).
3. **The alias question is open and the repo says so** — the `M3` and `Sperren` residues both end
   at it: two pointers to one object are indistinguishable, so a lock protecting `T` does not
   protect a second name for `T`. **This is the largest honest hole in the safety story**, and it
   is named in the tool's own output rather than in a footnote.
4. **48 of 263 refusal codes stand without a theorem**, and `state` is a refusal the grammar
   table has called uncovered for days pending an owner decision.

### The verdict

**As a research artefact it is unusually honest and unusually well instrumented.** The
discipline that produced it — a mark may fall, never rise; a number proves its denominator, not
its label; the guard gets checked before the tree — is doing real work: three of the four
defects in this audit were found *by* that discipline (running the emitted C through a sharper
tool, then asking what held the good cases in place), and my own three measurement errors were
caught by the same reflex.

**As a language for a user today it is small but real.** Programs print, link across units and
run; the minimum is five lines and a body; the errors teach. What it cannot yet do is protect a
program from aliasing, and it says so.

**The sentence I would put on it:** *Gabbro is further along in knowing what it has not proved
than most languages are in proving anything.* The risk that follows is the mirror image — a
project this good at naming its residues can spend a long time naming them. The three refusals
added today were not hard; they were simply never asked for, because the area **looked** finished
and nothing had put the emitted C in front of a stricter tool.

**The cheapest next measurement is the one that found them:** take an output the project already
produces, hand it to something that did not write it, and read what comes back.
